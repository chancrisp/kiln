# Changelog

All notable changes to Kiln are listed here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow
[Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [0.1.0] - Unreleased

The first version.

### Added

- `kiln-core`:
  - A format registry in `formats.toml` that rejects duplicate types, aliases and pairs when it loads.
  - Converters for images (built in), audio (built in), video (built in for MP4, MOV, M4V and MKV, FFmpeg for the rest), documents (Pandoc), office documents, spreadsheets, slides and PDF output (LibreOffice, optional), e-books (Calibre, optional) and PDF (built in). That makes 85 formats and 1,245 pairs.
  - Chains: a pair no single engine can do runs as two steps through a shared format, such as JPEG XL to JPG through PNG or MOBI to PDF through DOCX, inside one temporary folder.
  - Images in PNG, JPG, WEBP, BMP, GIF, TIFF, ICO, TGA, QOI and PPM, each checked by its signature before decoding. SVG is drawn by resvg, and camera RAW (DNG, CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, ORF, RW2, RAF, PEF, SRW) is decoded by Windows. JPEG XL goes through FFmpeg, and so does writing AVIF.
  - A photo's EXIF orientation is applied, so it comes out upright, and its colour profile is kept in JPG, PNG, TIFF and WEBP when it is 4 MB or smaller. A larger profile is left out and the conversion goes on.
  - WEBP is written lossy by libwebp, at the quality the preset or the quality setting gives, with transparency kept. An animated GIF converted to WEBP stays animated, with every frame, its timing and its loop count; a GIF with no loop count plays once.
  - Audio in WAV, MP3, FLAC, OGG, OPUS, M4A, AAC, AIFF, WMA, AC3 and WavPack. Video in MP4, M4V, MOV, MKV, WEBM, AVI, WMV, FLV, MPG, TS, 3GP and OGV, plus animated GIF, WEBP and APNG from video and back.
  - Subtitles in SRT, VTT and ASS, and the first subtitle track out of MKV and MP4.
  - Documents in Org, AsciiDoc, Typst, MediaWiki, Textile, DocBook, FictionBook and Jupyter notebooks, alongside Markdown, HTML, DOCX, ODT, EPUB, RST and LaTeX. Pages, WordPerfect, Visio, Publisher, Numbers and Keynote files are read through LibreOffice.
  - When Pandoc turns a document into DOCX, ODT, EPUB, PPTX, RTF, FB2 or IPYNB (a Markdown or HTML file, say), the pictures in the document's own folder and the folders inside it go in. A picture from a web address, or from outside that folder, becomes its alt text. Targets that only name their pictures, such as Markdown and HTML, keep the references as written.
  - A spreadsheet with several sheets converts to CSV as one file for each sheet, named after the file and the sheet, such as `book-Sales.csv`. A spreadsheet with one sheet makes `book.csv`.
  - PDF in process: its text out, its pages as images (drawn by Windows), images in as pages, and PDF merge, split and compress, with no engine needed. A PDF that needs a password is refused. The PDF tools refuse one with only an owner password, which they would write out with its restrictions gone; its text still converts.
  - A file built to wear Kiln out fails its one conversion with a plain sentence, and Kiln goes on. A PDF whose pages draw themselves or nest far deeper than any real one, and an SVG nested too deeply, whose effects are too heavy or that takes more than a minute to draw, are refused. An SVG that would draw at more than 50 megapixels is drawn smaller, in the same shape. Every thread that parses a user's file has a 64 MiB stack, the command line's work included.
  - When a conversion drops something, Kiln says so: `Keeps the first of 2 sound tracks.` for a video with several, `Subtitles aren't kept.` for a video going to another video, and `Keeps the text only: no pictures, tables or layout.` for a PDF made into a document, an e-book or slides. FFmpeg takes the first picture and the first sound track. The options sheet shows these before **Convert**, the row shows them as the job runs and once it is done, and the drag-wheel's notification adds them. Where the target is a PDF's text, it reads `DOCX (text only)`.
  - Quality settings: Small, High and Best presets, caps on resolution, frame rate and image size, audio bitrate and page DPI. `knobs` says which ones change the result of a pair, and `probe` and `estimate` describe the file and the likely result.
  - LibreOffice runs with a fresh, locked-down profile per job: no macros, no active content, no update check, and no wait on a sleeping default printer. It fetches nothing from the web, and it embeds no local file a document links to: an RTF's linked pictures are cut out of a copy before LibreOffice opens it, by what the file holds and not by its extension.
  - Cancelling a job stops the engine and every process it started.
  - File tools: compress, crop, trim, split and merge. Trim cuts silence from audio, and stretches that are both black and silent from video.
  - A worker pool with per-job progress and cancel.
  - Per-job temporary folders, output names that never replace anything, and safe handling of reserved Windows names. At start Kiln clears the working folders a crash or a kill left, unless a job is still using one. A folder that can't be written to fails its job at once, as `Kiln can't save in D:\Photos.`
- `kiln-media`, the audio engine, so audio converts with nothing installed:
  - Reads WAV (RF64 past 4 GB too), AIFF, FLAC, MP3, Ogg Vorbis, Opus (in WebM and Matroska too), M4A, AAC and ALAC, WMA, AC3 and WavPack, and the sound of MP4, M4V, MOV, MKV and WEBM video.
  - Writes WAV, AIFF, FLAC, MP3, Ogg Vorbis, Opus, M4A, AAC, WMA, AC3 and WavPack. Lossless conversions stay bit for bit at 16 and 24 bits.
  - Sound with a gap of 100 ms or more in its timestamps, as when a stretch is cut from an MKV, gets silence there and stays in step with the picture. FLAC is included, since its timestamps come from its frame headers. A gap gets at most 10 minutes of silence, and so does one file in all. A packet the decoder refuses is skipped, not filled.
  - MP3 is written by LAME, built as its own DLL, `lame_enc.dll`, which sits beside `kiln-app.exe` and `kiln.exe` and is loaded at run time. It is LGPL, so it is not linked into either.
  - AAC, M4A, WMA and stereo AC3 use the codecs in Windows, through Media Foundation. AC3 with 5.1 channels is written by Kiln's own encoder, from the A/52 standard. On an N edition of Windows these need the Media Feature Pack.
  - Presets (Small, High and Best), the steps of each format, an estimate of the result's size, and Fit under, which picks the setting that keeps a file below a size.
  - FFmpeg is now for video other than MP4, MOV, M4V and MKV, for the audio tools (Compress, Trim, Split and Merge), and for the sound of AVI, WMV, FLV, MPG, TS, 3GP and OGV.
  - A quality harness (`crates/kiln-media/harness`) that holds Kiln's audio to FFmpeg's, fuzzers for every reader (`crates/kiln-media/fuzz` and `tests/fuzz_lite.rs`), and Symphonia copied into `crates/kiln-media/vendor` with small fixes the fuzzers called for.
- Video, so MP4, MOV, M4V and MKV convert among themselves with nothing installed (`kiln-media`, `kiln-core` and the Windows app):
  - Kiln reads and writes the four containers itself, with Windows' codecs through Media Foundation and Direct3D 11 to decode and encode. Facts (length, size, frame rate, codec, rotation, HDR, pixel aspect, black bars) come from Kiln's own reading of the files, not ffprobe.
  - When the video and sound fit the new container and no setting changes them, they are copied without re-encoding (Original). A copy keeps the colour its source's container states. H.264 and HEVC go into any of the four, AV1 and VP9 into MKV only. AAC goes into any of the four, and Opus, Vorbis, FLAC and AC3 into MKV.
  - Otherwise the picture is written as H.264, or HEVC if chosen, on the graphics card where there is one and on the processor otherwise. Automatic falls back to the processor, and the finished row says `Encoded on the processor.` only after a real fallback. Output is written to a `.part` file in the job's working folder, with an MP4's samples waiting beside it, and renamed when complete. It is removed on error or cancel, a cancel while an MP4 is being finished included.
  - Phone video comes out upright. Pixels that aren't square keep their aspect. HDR stays HDR in HEVC on the graphics card, and becomes standard range in H.264 and in HEVC on the processor, because Windows' software HEVC encoder takes 8-bit video only. Nothing is made larger.
  - The sound keeps its start, and the container's creation date and title are kept.
  - **Video quality** as radio buttons, **Small**, **High**, **Best** and **Original (no re-encode)**, with the size it would come to and **More options**: Quality (every step), Resolution and Frame rate (lower steps only), Codec, Sound (Keep, or a bitrate) and Max size. Codec is disabled while the picture is copied.
  - Fit under on an encoder never delivers a file over the size: up to three passes, otherwise `MP4 can't go under 1.5 MB for this file.`
  - A row for Microsoft's video extensions names the formats that need one, as in `Needs Microsoft's AV1 Video Extensions to convert AV1 video to MP4, MOV or M4V.`, or `Needs Microsoft's HEVC Video Extensions to re-encode HEVC video.` where every format copies the picture. The row keeps **Convert**, since a copy needs no extension, and **Get it** in the panel opens the extension's Store page. A conversion that needs the extension fails with `Needs Microsoft's HEVC Video Extensions to read HEVC video.` and **Get it**. The same goes for AV1 and VP9. The row clears when the extension is installed, the next time Kiln's window is activated. Writing HEVC on a PC with no HEVC encoder needs the HEVC extension too. A file cut short, or with a broken container, says `This video is damaged and can't be read.`
  - FFmpeg is still used for WebM, AVI, WMV, FLV, MPG, TS, 3GP and OGV, GIFs, the video tools (Compress, Trim, Split, Merge and Crop) and subtitles. It is also the fallback for a codec Windows can't read (ProRes, for example), for one whose extension isn't installed, for HEVC on a PC with no HEVC encoder, for sound Kiln can't read, and on a PC without Media Foundation. The fallback follows the sheet's resolution, frame rate, codec, sound and Fit under, and writes the sound as Kiln does: copied where the new container holds it, else at the bitrate the size estimate counts.
  - Known limits: copying honours only an MP4's first edit, DTS sound can't go into MP4 or MKV without FFmpeg, AC-3 and E-AC-3 sound in MKV can't go into MP4, MOV or M4V without FFmpeg (E-AC-3 not into MKV either), a file cut exactly on a block boundary can't be told from a complete one, Fit under on a single pass through FFmpeg may refuse where two passes would fit.
  - The MP4 and MKV fact readers are fuzzed, as the audio readers are.
- `kiln` command line: `convert`, `tool` and `formats`, with `--preset`, `--quality` (JPG and WEBP), `--size`, `--fps`, `--audio-kbps` and `--dpi`.
- The Windows app:
  - A drop target and file list.
  - The glaze wheel, with Convert and Tools. The convert wheel keeps eight common targets close, and a More segment pours a second ring with every other format, grouped by kind and named (Images, Video, Audio, Documents). A lit format says what it is in the footer and on the options sheet, as in "WEBP: a small image for the web".
  - An options sheet: choosing a format, in the convert wheel or in the panel, opens it, as Tab and a right-click on a format do. It shows only the quality settings that fit the file, what the file is, what the conversion leaves out, and an estimate of the result, and its **Convert** converts. A pair with nothing to set opens it too. Its notes give way before Cancel and Convert in a short window.
  - Quality on the options sheet for audio that Kiln writes: **Small**, **High** and **Best** as radio buttons, the size it would come to, and **More options** for the quality's every step, the sample rate, the channels and Max size.
  - Rows for sound say what it is (`MP3 · 128 kbps · 3:41`) and, for a damaged file, an unsupported codec or a video with no sound, why it can't be converted.
  - A row for Windows' Media Feature Pack, where the codecs are missing: `Needs Windows' Media Feature Pack, which isn't installed.`, with **Get Media Feature Pack**, which opens Settings > Optional features.
  - Settings in three tabs, General, Quality and Helper apps, with quality defaults for video, images, audio and pages drawn as images. Labels say what they change, as in Sound quality and Sharpness (DPI).
  - Help: a **?** in the header, or F1, lists the ways in, where results go, and every keyboard shortcut. Icon-only controls have tooltips with their shortcuts, and a row's tooltip gives its whole line and full folder path.
  - Progress, results and errors shown in each row, plus a details dialog. The progress line glows and breathes while a job runs.
  - Pressed controls show a soft inner shadow, and the empty list's hint names the wheel keys.
  - One window: files dropped on the icon while Kiln is open go to the open window.
  - Other ways in: an **Add files** button in the empty window and the footer, and Ctrl+O, open the Windows Open dialog; Ctrl+V adds files copied in File Explorer; and right-clicking a row, the Menu key or Shift+F10 opens a native menu with the wheels, Export, Open, Show in folder and Remove. The empty window says what to do under its headline, the footer says what to do next once files are listed, and each wheel's title names its key.
  - Check for updates in Settings, the app's only network call, made only when pressed.
  - A settings panel and `%APPDATA%\Kiln\settings.toml`, covering the output folder, where new files go, theme, quality defaults and engine paths for FFmpeg, Pandoc, LibreOffice and Calibre.
  - Results are saved as soon as they are ready, next to the original or in the folder chosen in Settings, under a free name that never replaces a file (`photo (1).jpg`). Each is checked before Kiln lets go of its own copy: on the same drive the move is a rename of the one file, and from another drive the copy is compared byte for byte before Kiln's copy goes. When Kiln can't save in that folder, such as a camera card, the result goes to `Documents\Kiln results`, and the row says `Saved in Documents\Kiln results. Kiln can't save in DCIM.` When that can't be done either, Kiln keeps the result and tries again the next time it starts, and the row and a notification say `Couldn't save this. Kiln tries again the next time it starts.` and `Couldn't save photo.webp. Kiln tries again the next time it starts.` A close that was waiting for that save is called off, and the footer says the same instead of `Saving… Kiln will close when it's done.` A close waiting for a save that is cancelled is called off too, and the footer goes back to the count of files. The notification that tells a result is saved says `Saved photo.webp next to the original.`, and a click on it opens Explorer on the file.
  - `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Kiln\logs` holds `kiln.log`, which has a line for each failed conversion and rolls to `kiln.1.log` at 1 MB, and a crash file for each panic (the newest 10 are kept) with the problem, where it happened, a backtrace, Kiln's version and commit, and the Windows version. A panic in a conversion fails only that conversion. A panic in the window's code shows `Kiln ran into a problem and has to close. Your converted files are kept, and Kiln saves them the next time it starts. A report was saved in` the logs folder, and closes with the results kept.
  - At start, results left by a session that crashed, was killed, or ended with a sign-out or shutdown, and results an earlier version kept, are saved without a question, off the window's thread: next to their originals (or in the folder chosen in Settings when they were made), else in `Documents\Kiln results`. Nothing is moved over a file already there, and a running Kiln's own results are never touched. Kiln lets go of its own copy of a result it noted as saved only when the saved file holds the same bytes and is another file, outside the folders Kiln keeps results in; any other is saved under a free name. An InfoBar at the top of the window says it once, as `Saved 3 converted files from last time next to their originals.`; a result that can't be saved stays for the next start, as `1 converted file from last time couldn't be saved. Kiln tries again the next time it starts.` A quit removes only the folders that hold no result.
  - Light and dark themes that follow Windows.
  - Six more themes: Dracula, Gruvbox, Monokai, Nord, Solarized Dark and Solarized Light. Settings > Appearance > Theme lists them after System, Light and Dark, each with three small dots (its background, text and accent colours), and a choice applies at once. Each theme has one accent, a green or an aqua of its own, and passes the same contrast checks as Light and Dark. They are drawn on their own solid background, with no Mica. While a Windows contrast theme is on, Kiln uses its colours and the Theme note says so. `settings.toml` names them `dracula`, `gruvbox`, `monokai`, `nord`, `solarized-dark` and `solarized-light`.
  - Per-monitor DPI awareness and full keyboard control.
- Suggestions:
  - `kiln-core` gains `inspect`, which looks inside a file once, stopping each engine run or PDF read after 2 s: the size and look of a picture (photo or graphic), transparency and motion, audio and video details, black bars, and whether a PDF has text. `suggest` turns those facts into at most one conversion and one tool, each with a one-line reason and, where it can, an estimate.
  - Only targets whose engine is installed are suggested. For several files, a conversion is suggested only when every file gets the same answer.
  - `kiln suggest FILE...` prints each file's suggestion and the set's.
  - In the app, files are inspected on one low-priority thread as they are added. Each row's second line shows what Kiln found and the size, then the suggestion in the file's glaze colour, fading in over 160 ms.
  - The convert and Tools wheels mark the suggestion with a small celadon dot, on More and in the ring when it sits there. Opened from the keyboard, a wheel starts on the suggestion, and the footer gives the reason.
  - **Settings > General** gains **Suggestions**, On or Off. `settings.toml` gains `suggestions = true`.
- The drag-wheel:
  - Tapping Shift while dragging files anywhere in Windows opens the convert wheel under the pointer. A tap is a press and release within 400 ms with the mouse button down, so holding Shift still extends a selection and still makes File Explorer move a file. Tapping again swaps formats and tools, and the Tools key opens the tools ring directly.
  - The wheel is the in-window wheel's drawing in a window of its own, over any app. It opens centred on the pointer, kept on the pointer's monitor and over the taskbar too, but not over Kiln's own window or while a Kiln prompt is open. It fades out over 120 ms when it closes, and takes no drop while it fades. Its hub shows the files' kind, the file's size or the count of files, and the name of the slice under the pointer, and that slice lifts as it fills solid with celadon. Files that can't become anything show `No formats` (`No tools` on the tools ring), with the reason in a caption under the wheel.
  - Dropping on a format converts at once with that format's defaults, without bringing Kiln's window forward, and dropping on a tool runs it. The original is never moved. Every conversion from the drag-wheel is saved as soon as it is ready, as any is. A drop onto a row whose result is being saved is refused, and Kiln says `Saving…`; drop again once the save is done.
  - One notification for each drop names the result, as in `Converted photo.jpg to WEBP.`, `Converted 2 of 3 files to WEBP.` or `Compressed 3 files.`, and a failure gives the row's reason. It comes once the results are saved and says where they went, as in `Saved photo.webp next to the original.`, and what the conversion left out follows. Clicking a notification about saved files opens Explorer on them; any other opens Kiln on the row.
  - A finished drop's row, and the inspector and the row's menu, offer **Options**, which opens the options sheet and converts again. The old result is kept until the new one is done, and a failed or cancelled redo leaves it as it was.
  - **Settings > Drag-wheel** has **Open the wheel anywhere while dragging a file**, on by default. While it is on, closing the window hides Kiln, and an icon in the notification area, there while the window is hidden, opens it again. A job still running goes on while Kiln is hidden. The icon's menu has **Open Kiln**, **Drag-wheel** and **Quit Kiln**, and the icon returns when Explorer restarts. Quitting asks only while files are converting. When Windows refuses the drag-wheel's hooks, closing the window quits. Kiln never holds up signing out or shutting down; a result it hasn't saved yet is kept and saved at the next start.
  - `settings.toml` gains `drag_wheel = true`.
- A new look: previews, a colour for each kind of file, a panel for the selected file, and smoother motion.
- Packaging: an MSI installer, a portable ZIP and optional code signing.
- The installer adds Kiln to Explorer's Open with and Send to.
- CI on windows-latest.
- `settings.toml` gains `save_to`, the folder new files go to. Unset, they go next to the original. The older `output_folder` is still read and written, and no longer used.
- While the drag-wheel is on, Kiln starts with Windows: turning it on writes the value `Kiln` under `HKCU\Software\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run`, `"<kiln-app.exe>" --background`, which Task Manager lists under Startup apps; turning it off removes it. Kiln removes it only when it names this `kiln-app.exe`, and each start with the drag-wheel on points it at this copy again if it names another path, as after a portable folder moved. The registry is written off the window's thread.
- `kiln-app.exe --background` starts Kiln in the notification area with no window shown and the drag-wheel watching. Opening Kiln again shows the window. Started this way with the drag-wheel off, Kiln removes its start-up entry and ends; with a `settings.toml` it can't read, it shows its window and the reason instead. Files given with `--background` open the window as usual, and flags Kiln doesn't know are ignored.
- The MSI's uninstall runs `kiln-app.exe --forget-startup`, which removes the start-up entry if it is Kiln's own, for the account that runs the uninstall. A failure never stops the uninstall.
- HEIC and AVIF are read by Windows, through Microsoft's HEIF Image Extensions (with the HEVC Video Extensions) and AV1 Video Extension, so they convert in process to every type the image backend writes, and on to PDF, AVIF and JPEG XL through PNG. Windows turns them upright, and Kiln keeps the colour profile stored in the file and reads the size for the row from the file's header. Where Windows can't read one, for any reason, FFmpeg reads it into a PNG in the job's folder, and the image backend goes on from there; a picture with transparency, which Windows' decoders drop, goes to FFmpeg first, and a transparent AVIF is refused with `Needs FFmpeg to keep this AVIF's transparency.` when FFmpeg is missing, rather than saved with its background filled in. With neither, the job says `Needs Microsoft's HEIF Image and HEVC Video Extensions, or FFmpeg.` (or `Needs Microsoft's AV1 Video Extension, or FFmpeg.` for AVIF), and its Get it link opens the page of the extension Windows lacks. `KILN_HIDE_WIC=1`, a test hook for debug builds, acts as if Windows had neither decoder.
- **Make smaller** for photos to a size (`kiln tool smaller --max-size 1MB`), in the same format, as `name (smaller).ext`. A JPG or WEBP takes the highest quality from 40 to 92 that fits, found in at most six trial encodes held in memory; when even 40 is over, the picture is scaled down by the square root of how far over it was and tried again, at most three times. A PNG is scaled down until it fits and keeps every colour. With a long side (`--long-side`) the picture is brought down to it first. The photo keeps its colour profile, and its Exif, XMP and IPTC with the orientation set to upright, since the pixels are turned, and without the Exif's old thumbnail; with Remove location data (`--remove-location`) only the profile stays. Kiln never saves a file over the size or larger than the original: a photo already under the size says `This file is already smaller than 1 MB.` unless a long side it is over was asked for too, when it is still brought down to it, and a size it can't reach saves nothing and says `Can't get this under 1 MB. The smallest Kiln can make is about 2.3 MB.` An animated PNG or WEBP says `Kiln can't make animated pictures smaller.`
- **Make smaller** for MP4, MOV, M4V and MKV to a size (`kiln tool smaller --max-size 10MB`), in the same container, as `name (smaller).ext`, on Kiln's own engine with no FFmpeg. The picture gets the bitrate Fit under finds for the size, encoded on the graphics card as conversions are; a first pass that lands over the size is encoded again with the size model corrected by how far it missed. The sound is copied where the container holds it, unless that leaves too little for the picture, when it is encoded at the Small preset's bitrate. Its options can cap the picture's short side (720 for 720p) and leave the sound out. Kiln never saves a file over the size or larger than the original: a video already under the size says `This file is already smaller than 10 MB.`, unless a smaller picture or no sound was asked for too, and a size it can't reach saves nothing and says `Can't get this under 1 MB. The smallest Kiln can make is about 2.3 MB.` A video whose length isn't known says `Couldn't tell how long this file is.` before anything is encoded. With no size, the picture is encoded at the Small step and kept only if it is smaller. A video job can now leave the sound out when asked (`SoundChoice::Off`); otherwise the sound is never dropped. `KILN_FIT_FIRST_PASS_OVER`, a test hook for debug builds, multiplies Fit under's first bitrate.
- **Make smaller** for PDF to a size (`kiln tool smaller --max-size 5MB`), in process, as `name (smaller).pdf`. The PDF is first tidied: objects nothing uses and empty pieces of page content are dropped, and the rest is compressed and packed into object streams. Until it fits, its photos are then saved as JPG at quality 70, 55 and 40, and then the same with photos over 2000, 1400 and 1000 pixels on the long side brought down to that; it stops at the first step that fits. The smallest step is tried first after the tidy, so a size it can't reach is known after one more trial. A photo whose soft mask has a matte colour keeps its size, and a JPG photo with decode parameters is left as it is. A photo is an 8-bit RGB or grey picture of at least 256 x 256 pixels stored as JPG, or as Flate without a soft mask, which becomes a JPG; a photo is replaced only when the new one is smaller. Text, vector drawing, fonts, masks and every other stream are carried byte for byte, and every trial is held in memory. A photo's data is read no further than its picture needs, and a photo claiming over 50 megapixels is left as it is. Kiln never saves a file over the size or larger than the original: a PDF already under the size says `This file is already smaller than 5 MB.`, and a size it can't reach saves nothing and says `Can't get this under 1 MB. The smallest Kiln can make is about 2.3 MB.` With no size, it is tidied and its JPG photos saved at quality 70 where that saves a tenth, as Compress did.
- **Resize** for photos (`kiln tool resize --long-side 1920`) brings the long side down to the size given and saves `name (1920 px).ext` in the same format at the Photos quality, turned upright, with its colour profile and its details (or without them, with `--remove-location`). It never enlarges: a photo already that small says `This picture's long side is already 1280 px.` A HEIC or AVIF becomes a JPG. Without a size it says `Resize needs a size for the long side.`, and an animated picture says `Kiln can't resize animated pictures.`
- **Remove location data** for photos (`kiln tool remove-location`) saves `name (no location).ext` without where the photo was taken and the camera details. A JPG, PNG, WEBP or GIF is rewritten around its picture, which is copied byte for byte: Exif (with its GPS block, make, model and serial numbers), XMP, IPTC, comments, text chunks and blocks Kiln doesn't know go, while the colour profile, the orientation (kept as an Exif block holding only that) and a GIF's frames and loop count stay. Anything after a JPG's end, such as a phone's extra pictures with their own Exif, goes too. A TIFF or BMP is saved again with the same pixels, and a HEIC or AVIF becomes a JPG at the Photos quality. A photo with nothing to take out says `There's no location data or camera details to remove.`
- FFmpeg comes with Kiln, as a separate LGPL program in `ffmpeg\bin` beside the executables, in the MSI and the portable ZIP, with `ffmpeg\LICENSE.txt`, `ffmpeg\BUILD.txt` (its configuration and the commits it was built from) and `ffmpeg\SOURCE.txt`, which says where its source is. Each release gains `Kiln-<version>-ffmpeg-source.zip`. The build is FFmpeg 7.1 from BtbN's FFmpeg-Builds scripts, by the workflow `.github/workflows/ffmpeg-lgpl.yml`, which refuses a build with GPL or nonfree parts or without the encoders Kiln uses.
